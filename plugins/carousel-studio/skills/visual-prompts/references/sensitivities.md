# Sensitivities: what a picture must not show

Audiences across the Arab region share many norms and differ on others, so the rules below are conservative on purpose. The sensitivity gate (SEN1) checks the finished post against the flags of the occasion and the copy; this page keeps the **pictures** inside the same limits before they are made. When a request crosses a line, decline in one sentence, say why, and offer a safe alternative.

## 1. Never, on any promotional slide or plate
| Do not show | Because | Safe alternative |
|---|---|---|
| verses of the Quran, in any script or calligraphy, or the Shahada | sacred text on a sales message offends | a neutral pattern; put your own words in the type layer |
| the Saudi flag (it carries the Shahada) | the same reason; it is also restricted on commercial goods | the country's colours as a colour field |
| the Kaaba or a mosque as a selling backdrop | worship places are not decoration for a sale | a neutral interior, an abstract pattern; the Kaaba or mosques may appear in non-promotional educational content when the subject is the place itself |
| depictions of the Prophet, his family or companions | forbidden for most Muslims | none: describe in words |
| maps of disputed borders or regions | political claim | no map, or a plain abstract shape |
| the Gulf named anything but «الخليج العربي» in a post for a GCC audience | audience expectation (and the SEN1 gate) | use the term |

## 2. People and modesty
- Prefer plates without people. A close-up face is where models fail (uncanny skin, wrong hands) and where rights questions begin.
- Never a real public figure's likeness, never a lookalike request, never a child.
- Clothing and poses stay modest and everyday for a broad regional audience; no suggestive content. For the Gulf and Egypt's conservative audiences this also means no bare shoulders or tight clothing in staged scenes.
- No "Arab" clichés by default (camels, endless dunes, oud smoke, belly-dance imagery) unless the brand is about heritage and the user asked; contemporary interiors, streets and objects are usually truer.
- A photo of an identifiable person belongs to them: use only the user's own, or ones they have written permission for, and say that the image goes to the provider.

## 3. Things most regional brands avoid showing
Alcohol, pork products, gambling, tobacco and vaping, nightlife imagery, and religious symbols of other faiths used decoratively. If the user's business legally sells one of these, ask who the audience is before generating anything.

## 4. Politics and conflict
No flags of disputed entities, no military or protest imagery, no images of conflict zones, no maps, no political slogans, even as texture. National days are celebrated with pride, colour and local landmarks; nothing that makes a territorial claim. Solemn days (remembrance, Ashura) get quiet imagery or none.

## 5. Occasion symbols
Lanterns, crescents, dates and warm lamplight are welcome for Ramadan and Eid greetings and for offers tied to the season, with no religious text on them. Christmas and Coptic occasions: a short respectful greeting, no sales imagery built on religious scenes. Occasion rules and flags: the `content-plan` skill's occasion rules; they travel with the slot (`noQuranOnPromo`, `noShahada`, `noSaudiFlagOnPromo`, `noKaabaForSelling`, `noDisputedMaps`, `term`).

## 6. Intellectual property
No logos, trademarks, packaging of known brands, copyrighted characters, or another artist's recognisable style by name. Ask for an original scene. A generator's watermark or signature is a reject. Screenshots of other people's posts are never plates.

## 7. Honesty
AI imagery is labelled: the asset is `aiGenerated: true`, the kit lists it, and the user is told to switch on Instagram's AI label. A plate is not presented as a photograph of a real place, event or person, and a before-and-after or a result is never illustrated with generated pictures posing as proof.

## 8. When unsure
Ask the user who the audience is (country, age, how conservative) and what the picture is for, then pick the safer option. Never test a borderline prompt "to see what comes out": each call costs credits and sends the prompt to a third party.
